## **Marbury v. Madison (1803)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Established the principle of **judicial review**, giving the Supreme Court the power to strike down laws deemed unconstitutional.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - The case arose from a dispute over judicial appointments at the end of John Adams's presidency.  
  - Chief Justice **John Marshall** ruled that the Judiciary Act of 1789 conflicted with the Constitution.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Cemented the judiciary's role as a coequal branch of government.  
  - Strengthened the concept of checks and balances.

## **McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Confirmed the federal government’s supremacy over states and upheld the constitutionality of the **Second Bank of the United States**.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Maryland attempted to tax the federal bank.  
  - The court, led by Chief Justice **John Marshall**, ruled that Congress had implied powers under the Necessary and Proper Clause.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Strengthened federal authority.  
  - Set a precedent for expansive interpretation of federal powers.  

---

## **Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Ruled that African Americans, whether free or enslaved, were not U.S. citizens and could not sue in federal court.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Dred Scott, an enslaved man, sued for his freedom after living in a free territory.  
  - Chief Justice **Roger Taney** argued that Congress could not ban slavery in the territories.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Deepened sectional tensions.  
  - Invalidated the **Missouri Compromise** and contributed to the Civil War.  

---

## **Plessy v. Ferguson (1896)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Upheld the constitutionality of racial segregation under the doctrine of **"separate but equal."**  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Homer Plessy, a mixed-race man, was arrested for sitting in a "whites-only" train car.  
  - The court ruled segregation was permissible if facilities were equal.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Legitimized Jim Crow laws.  
  - Institutionalized racial segregation for decades.  

---

## **Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka (1954)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Overturned **Plessy v. Ferguson**, declaring that racial segregation in public schools violated the **Equal Protection Clause** of the 14th Amendment.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - The case consolidated several lawsuits by African American families challenging school segregation.  
  - Chief Justice **Earl Warren** led a unanimous decision.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Marked a major victory for the Civil Rights Movement.  
  - Paved the way for desegregation across the U.S.

## **Miranda v. Arizona (1966)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Established the **Miranda Rights**, requiring law enforcement to inform suspects of their rights before interrogation.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Ernesto Miranda’s confession was used in court without him being informed of his right to remain silent or have an attorney.  
  - The court ruled that this violated the 5th Amendment's protection against self-incrimination.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Strengthened protections for individuals in police custody.  
  - "You have the right to remain silent..." became a standard legal procedure.

## **Roe v. Wade (1973)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Recognized a woman’s constitutional right to privacy, including the right to choose an abortion.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Jane Roe (a pseudonym) challenged Texas laws banning abortion.  
  - The court ruled that states could not ban abortion outright but could regulate it after the first trimester.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Polarized public opinion on abortion.  
  - Became a cornerstone of debates over reproductive rights until it was overturned by **Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022)**.

## **United States v. Nixon (1974)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Limited presidential power by ruling that **executive privilege** is not absolute, particularly in criminal investigations.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - During the Watergate scandal, President **Richard Nixon** refused to turn over White House tapes.  
  - The court unanimously ruled that Nixon must release the tapes.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Led to Nixon’s resignation.  
  - Reinforced the principle that no one, not even the president, is above the law.

## **Regents of the University of California v. Bakke (1978)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Addressed affirmative action in college admissions, ruling that racial quotas are unconstitutional but race can be considered as a factor.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Allan Bakke, a white applicant, argued he was denied admission to medical school due to racial quotas favoring minority applicants.  
  - The court issued a split decision, upholding affirmative action but rejecting quotas.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Set a precedent for affirmative action policies.  
  - Sparked ongoing debates about diversity in education and employment.

## **Bush v. Gore (2000)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Resolved the 2000 presidential election by halting Florida’s recount, effectively awarding the presidency to **George W. Bush**.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - The court ruled that varying recount methods violated the Equal Protection Clause.  
  - The decision was controversial and split 5-4.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Undermined public confidence in the impartiality of the judiciary.  
  - Highlighted flaws in the U.S. electoral process.

## **Obergefell v. Hodges (2015)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Legalized same-sex marriage nationwide, ruling that bans on same-sex marriage violated the 14th Amendment.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - James Obergefell sued after being unable to list his name as a surviving spouse on his partner’s death certificate.  
  - The court ruled that marriage is a fundamental right.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Landmark victory for LGBTQ+ rights.  
  - Ended decades of legal battles over marriage equality.
