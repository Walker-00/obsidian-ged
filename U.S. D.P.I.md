## **Missouri Compromise (1820)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Addressed the balance of power between free and slave states as the U.S. expanded westward.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Missouri entered the Union as a slave state; Maine entered as a free state.  
  - Slavery was prohibited in territories north of the 36°30′ latitude line.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Temporarily eased sectional tensions.  
  - Highlighted the growing divide between North and South over slavery.

---

## **Compromise of 1850**  
- **Overview:**  
  A series of laws aimed at diffusing tensions between free and slave states after the Mexican-American War.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - California was admitted as a free state.  
  - The Fugitive Slave Act was strengthened, requiring escaped slaves to be returned.  
  - Popular sovereignty was implemented in Utah and New Mexico territories.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Delayed the Civil War but exacerbated tensions over slavery.  
  - The Fugitive Slave Act angered abolitionists and fueled resistance.

---

## **Homestead Act (1862)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Encouraged westward expansion by offering 160 acres of public land to settlers for a small fee.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Applicants had to live on the land for five years and improve it to gain ownership.  
  - Benefited farmers, immigrants, and freed slaves but displaced Native Americans.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Spurred agricultural growth and westward migration.  
  - Contributed to the displacement and marginalization of Native American tribes.

## **New Deal (1933–1939)**  
- **Overview:**  
  A series of programs, public works projects, and reforms launched by President **Franklin D. Roosevelt** to combat the Great Depression.  

- **Key Programs:**  
  - **Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC):** Provided jobs for young men in environmental conservation.  
  - **Public Works Administration (PWA):** Funded large-scale infrastructure projects.  
  - **Social Security Act (1935):** Established a safety net for the elderly, unemployed, and disabled.  
  - **Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA):** Modernized the Tennessee Valley through electrification and flood control.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Restored public confidence in the government.  
  - Reshaped the role of federal government in economic and social welfare.  

---

## **Fair Deal (1949)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Proposed by President **Harry S. Truman** to extend New Deal reforms and promote social and economic equality.  

- **Key Proposals:**  
  - Expanded Social Security benefits.  
  - Advocated for national healthcare.  
  - Pushed for civil rights legislation and minimum wage increases.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Some measures (e.g., minimum wage hike) were enacted, but others (e.g., healthcare) faced strong opposition.

## **Square Deal (1901–1909)**  
- **Overview:**  
  President **Theodore Roosevelt’s** domestic policy aimed at protecting consumers, regulating corporations, and conserving natural resources.  

- **Key Initiatives:**  
  - **Trust-Busting:** Broke up monopolies like Standard Oil to ensure fair competition.  
  - **Pure Food and Drug Act (1906):** Regulated food safety.  
  - **Conservation:** Established national parks and protected public lands.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Laid the groundwork for modern consumer and environmental protections.  
  - Strengthened the federal government’s regulatory role.

## **Great Society (1964–1965)**  
- **Overview:**  
  President **Lyndon B. Johnson’s** domestic agenda to eliminate poverty and racial injustice.  

- **Key Programs:**  
  - **Medicare and Medicaid:** Provided healthcare to the elderly and low-income individuals.  
  - **Civil Rights Act (1964):** Banned discrimination based on race, color, religion, or gender.  
  - **Voting Rights Act (1965):** Outlawed discriminatory voting practices.  
  - **War on Poverty:** Included programs like Head Start and Job Corps.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Significantly reduced poverty and improved access to education and healthcare.  
  - Advanced civil rights but faced criticism for increasing government spending.  

---

## **Reaganomics (1980s)**  
- **Overview:**  
  President **Ronald Reagan’s** economic policies focused on tax cuts, deregulation, and reducing government spending.  

- **Key Principles:**  
  - **Supply-Side Economics:** Lower taxes would stimulate economic growth.  
  - Deregulation of industries like banking and telecommunications.  
  - Cuts to social programs while increasing military spending.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Led to economic recovery in the short term but widened income inequality.  
  - Contributed to a significant increase in the national debt.

## **Affordable Care Act (2010)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Also known as **"Obamacare,"** this legislation aimed to expand healthcare coverage and reduce costs.  

- **Key Features:**  
  - Required individuals to have health insurance or face penalties (individual mandate).  
  - Expanded Medicaid coverage.  
  - Prohibited insurers from denying coverage based on pre-existing conditions.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Increased the number of insured Americans.  
  - Sparked political debates over healthcare reform.

## **Indian Removal Act (1830)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Authorized President **Andrew Jackson** to relocate Native American tribes from the southeastern U.S. to lands west of the Mississippi River.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Led to the forced removal of tribes, including the **Cherokee**, **Choctaw**, and **Creek**, via the **Trail of Tears**.  
  - Promised compensation and land in return, but promises were often unfulfilled.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Resulted in the death and displacement of thousands of Native Americans.  
  - Opened up land for white settlers but left a legacy of injustice.  

---

## **Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Allowed settlers in Kansas and Nebraska territories to decide whether to allow slavery, repealing the **Missouri Compromise**.  

- **Key Facts:**  
  - Introduced the concept of **popular sovereignty**.  
  - Sparked violent clashes between pro-slavery and anti-slavery factions (known as **Bleeding Kansas**).  

- **Impact:**  
  - Deepened sectional tensions and hastened the Civil War.  
  - Undermined the balance of power between free and slave states.
