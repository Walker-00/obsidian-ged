## **The American Revolution (1775–1783)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** British taxation without representation (e.g., Stamp Act, Tea Act), colonial desire for self-governance, and Enlightenment ideas.
- **Effect:** The United States gained independence and established a democratic government based on a constitution.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Key Events:**
  - **1773:** Boston Tea Party protested the Tea Act.
  - **1775:** Battles of Lexington and Concord marked the start of the war.
  - **1776:** Declaration of Independence, authored primarily by **Thomas Jefferson**, formally declared the colonies independent from Britain.
  - **1781:** Victory at Yorktown with help from French forces.
  - **1783:** Treaty of Paris officially ended the war.
- **Role of Enlightenment:**
  - Philosophies from thinkers like **John Locke** inspired ideas about natural rights and self-governance.

### **Key Figures**
- **George Washington:** Commander of the Continental Army, later became the first U.S. President.
- **Thomas Jefferson:** Principal author of the Declaration of Independence.
- **Benjamin Franklin:** Diplomat who secured French support for the war.
- **King George III:** British monarch during the revolution.

### **Key Documents**
- **Declaration of Independence (1776):** Outlined grievances against Britain and principles of freedom.
- **Articles of Confederation:** First governing document of the U.S., later replaced by the Constitution due to its weaknesses.

---

## **The U.S. Constitution (1787)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** The failure of the Articles of Confederation to create a strong federal government.
- **Effect:** The creation of a federal system with checks and balances.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Constitutional Convention (1787):**
  - Held in Philadelphia to address the weaknesses of the Articles.
  - Delegates, including **James Madison** ("Father of the Constitution"), debated how to balance power between states and the federal government.
- **Compromises:**
  - **The Great Compromise:** Created a bicameral legislature (House of Representatives and Senate).
  - **Three-Fifths Compromise:** Counted enslaved individuals as three-fifths of a person for representation and taxation.

### **Key Features**
- **Separation of Powers:** Divided government into executive, legislative, and judicial branches.
- **Bill of Rights (1791):** Added to protect individual freedoms (e.g., speech, religion).

### **Key Figures**
- **George Washington:** Presided over the convention.
- **Alexander Hamilton:** Advocated for a strong central government.
- **James Madison:** Drafted much of the Constitution and later the Bill of Rights.

---

## **The Civil War (1861–1865)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Conflicts over slavery, state rights, and economic differences between the North and South.
- **Effect:** The abolition of slavery (13th Amendment) and preservation of the Union.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Key Events:**
  - **1860:** Abraham Lincoln's election prompted Southern states to secede.
  - **1861:** Confederate forces attacked Fort Sumter, starting the war.
  - **1863:** Emancipation Proclamation freed enslaved individuals in Confederate states.
  - **1865:** The war ended with the surrender at Appomattox Court House.
- **Turning Points:**
  - Battle of Gettysburg (1863): Major Union victory.
  - Sherman's March to the Sea (1864): Destroyed Southern infrastructure.

### **Key Figures**
- **Abraham Lincoln:** U.S. President; issued the Emancipation Proclamation.
- **Ulysses S. Grant:** Union general; later became U.S. President.
- **Robert E. Lee:** Confederate general.
- **Frederick Douglass:** Former enslaved individual who became an abolitionist leader.

### **Key Amendments**
- **13th Amendment (1865):** Abolished slavery.
- **14th Amendment (1868):** Granted citizenship to all born in the U.S.
- **15th Amendment (1870):** Prohibited voter discrimination based on race.

---

## **Industrial Revolution (late 18th–19th centuries)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Technological innovations, population growth, and the need for efficient production.
- **Effect:** Urbanization, economic growth, and significant social changes.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Technological Advancements:**
  - Steam engines revolutionized transportation (e.g., railroads) and industry.
  - Innovations like the spinning jenny and power loom boosted textile production.
- **Social Changes:**
  - Migration to urban centers for factory jobs.
  - Growth of a working-class population; poor working conditions led to labor unions.

### **Key Figures**
- **James Watt:** Improved the steam engine.
- **Eli Whitney:** Invented the cotton gin, increasing demand for enslaved labor in the South.
- **Andrew Carnegie:** Industrialist who dominated the steel industry.

---

## **World War I (1914–1918)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism (MAIN).
- **Effect:** Treaty of Versailles redrew European borders and set conditions for World War II.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Key Events:**
  - Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand (1914) sparked the war.
  - Trench warfare led to a prolonged and deadly conflict.
  - The U.S. entered in 1917 after the sinking of the Lusitania and the Zimmerman Telegram.
- **End of the War:**
  - Treaty of Versailles (1919): Imposed harsh reparations on Germany.

### **Key Figures**
- **Woodrow Wilson:** U.S. President; proposed the **Fourteen Points** and the League of Nations.
- **Kaiser Wilhelm II:** German Emperor during the war.
- **Vladimir Lenin:** Leader of the Bolshevik Revolution, which led to Russia's exit from the war.

---

## **Great Depression (1929–1939)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Stock market crash (1929), bank failures, and poor economic policies.
- **Effect:** Massive unemployment and poverty; led to New Deal reforms.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Economic Collapse:**
  - Speculative investments and margin buying led to the crash.
  - Bank runs caused financial institutions to fail.
- **Government Response:**
  - President **Herbert Hoover** took limited action.
  - **Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal** introduced programs like Social Security and the CCC.

### **Key Figures**
- **Franklin D. Roosevelt:** U.S. President who implemented the New Deal.
- **John Maynard Keynes:** Economist advocating for government intervention during economic downturns.

---

## **The Progressive Era (1890s–1920s)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Social and economic issues during the Industrial Revolution (e.g., child labor, poor working conditions, corruption in politics).
- **Effect:** Reforms in labor laws, women’s suffrage, and government transparency; the rise of progressivism.
- **Key Events:**
  - Muckraking journalism exposed societal issues.
  - The Temperance Movement aimed to reduce alcohol consumption.
  - Women's suffrage campaigns gained momentum.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Social Movements:** 
  - Activists like **Jane Addams** founded settlement houses to help immigrants and the poor (e.g., Hull House in Chicago).
  - Labor unions protested unfair wages and working conditions (e.g., **American Federation of Labor**).
- **Political Reforms:**
  - **Initiative, Referendum, Recall:** Empowered citizens to propose laws, vote directly on policies, or remove officials.
  - The **17th Amendment (1913):** Allowed for the direct election of U.S. senators, reducing political corruption.

### **Key Figures**
- **Theodore Roosevelt:**
  - Advocated for the "Square Deal" to protect consumers, regulate businesses, and conserve natural resources.
  - Broke up monopolies using antitrust laws.
- **Upton Sinclair:**
  - Wrote *The Jungle* (1906), exposing unsanitary conditions in the meatpacking industry, leading to the **Pure Food and Drug Act** and **Meat Inspection Act**.
- **Susan B. Anthony & Elizabeth Cady Stanton:**
  - Leaders of the suffrage movement, culminating in the **19th Amendment (1920)**, granting women the right to vote.

### **Key Laws and Amendments**
- **16th Amendment (1913):** Established a federal income tax.
- **18th Amendment (1919):** Prohibited alcohol (led to Prohibition, later repealed by the 21st Amendment in 1933).
- **19th Amendment (1920):** Granted women the right to vote.

## **Civil Rights Movement (1950s–1960s)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** Continued racial segregation and discrimination, especially in the South, after the abolition of slavery.
- **Effect:** Landmark legislation, such as the **Civil Rights Act of 1964** and the **Voting Rights Act of 1965**, aimed to dismantle systemic racism.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Peaceful Protests:**
  - Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955–56): Sparked by **Rosa Parks** refusing to give up her bus seat.
  - March on Washington (1963): Over 250,000 people gathered, where **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.** delivered his iconic "I Have a Dream" speech.
- **Legal Battles:**
  - **Brown v. Board of Education (1954):** Supreme Court ruled that segregation in public schools was unconstitutional.
  - Freedom Riders (1961): Integrated groups rode buses into the South to challenge segregation laws.

### **Key Figures**
- **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.:**
  - Advocated for nonviolent protest; led the **Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC)**.
  - Key figure in organizing the March on Washington.
- **Malcolm X:**
  - Initially promoted Black separatism and later advocated for racial equality by any means necessary.
- **Thurgood Marshall:**
  - Lawyer for the **NAACP**, successfully argued *Brown v. Board of Education*; later became the first African American Supreme Court Justice.

### **Key Laws**
- **Civil Rights Act of 1964:**
  - Outlawed discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
  - Ended segregation in public places.
- **Voting Rights Act of 1965:**
  - Eliminated discriminatory practices, such as literacy tests, that prevented African Americans from voting.

## **Prohibition (1920–1933)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** The Temperance Movement claimed alcohol caused social problems like crime, poverty, and domestic abuse.
- **Effect:** The **18th Amendment** banned alcohol production, transportation, and sale, but it led to widespread illegal activity (e.g., bootlegging and speakeasies).

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Temperance Movement:**
  - Led by groups like the **Women's Christian Temperance Union (WCTU)** and activists such as **Frances Willard**.
- **Volstead Act (1919):**
  - Enforced Prohibition by defining "intoxicating liquors."
- **Resistance:** 
  - Organized crime flourished, with figures like **Al Capone** profiting from illegal alcohol sales.
  - Public frustration grew as enforcement was ineffective, and many ignored the ban.

### **Key Figures**
- **Frances Willard:** Leader in the Temperance Movement.
- **Carrie Nation:** Known for destroying saloons with a hatchet.
- **Al Capone:** A notorious gangster who capitalized on the illegal alcohol trade.

### **End of Prohibition**
- **21st Amendment (1933):** Repealed the 18th Amendment, ending Prohibition.

## **World War II (1939–1945)**

### **Overview**
- **Cause:** The Treaty of Versailles (1919) left Germany economically and politically unstable, enabling the rise of Adolf Hitler and fascism. Expansionist policies by Axis powers (Germany, Italy, and Japan) led to global conflict.
- **Effect:** The war reshaped the global political landscape, leading to the Cold War and the establishment of the United Nations.

---

### **Details: How It Happened**
- **Major Events:**
  - 1939: Germany invaded Poland, prompting Britain and France to declare war.
  - 1941: Japan attacked Pearl Harbor, bringing the United States into the war.
  - 1944: D-Day (Allied invasion of Normandy) marked a turning point in the European theater.
  - 1945: Germany surrendered; the U.S. dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, leading to Japan’s surrender.
- **Key Strategies:**
  - Blitzkrieg: Germany's "lightning war" strategy.
  - Island Hopping: U.S. strategy in the Pacific to capture key islands leading to Japan.

### **Key Figures**
- **Winston Churchill:** British Prime Minister; led Britain through the war.
- **Franklin D. Roosevelt:** U.S. President during most of the war.
- **Adolf Hitler:** German dictator; leader of the Nazi Party.
- **Joseph Stalin:** Soviet leader; part of the Allied Powers.
- **Dwight D. Eisenhower:** Supreme Allied Commander in Europe.
- **Harry Truman:** U.S. President who made the decision to use atomic bombs.

### **Allies and Axis Powers**
- **Allies:** United States, Britain, Soviet Union, China, and others.
- **Axis:** Germany, Italy, Japan.

### **Effects**
- Creation of the **United Nations** to promote peace.
- Start of the **Cold War** due to tensions between the U.S. and Soviet Union.
