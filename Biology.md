# Notes: GED Biology

## **Cell Biology**

### **Cell Theory**
- **Cause:** Early scientists observed cells under microscopes and proposed that living organisms are made of cells.
- **Effect:** The development of cell theory laid the foundation for modern biology.
- **Key Points:**
  - **All living things are composed of cells.**
  - **The cell is the basic unit of structure and function in living organisms.**
  - **All cells come from pre-existing cells.**
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Robert Hooke:** First to coin the term "cell" after observing cork cells under a microscope.
  - **Matthias Schleiden & Theodor Schwann:** Proposed the idea that plants and animals are made up of cells.
  - **Rudolf Virchow:** Proposed that all cells come from pre-existing cells.

### **Structure of the Cell**
- **Cause:** Advancements in microscopy led to the discovery of cell organelles and structures.
- **Effect:** Understanding cell structures allowed for deeper insights into cellular functions and life processes.
- **Key Organelles:**
  - **Nucleus:** Contains genetic material (DNA) and controls cellular activities.
  - **Mitochondria:** Produces energy (ATP) for the cell, often called the "powerhouse."
  - **Ribosomes:** Synthesizes proteins.
  - **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER):** Smooth ER (lipid synthesis) and Rough ER (protein synthesis).
  - **Golgi Apparatus:** Modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids.
  - **Lysosomes:** Digest waste materials and cellular debris.
  - **Chloroplasts (in plant cells):** Conducts photosynthesis.

![[Plant-Cell-and-Animal-Cell-Diagram.webp|]]

---

### **Cell Division**
- **Cause:** Cells must divide to grow, repair tissues, and reproduce.
- **Effect:** The process of cell division ensures the continuation of life.
- **Key Types of Cell Division:**
  - **Mitosis:** Division of somatic cells (body cells). Results in two identical daughter cells.
    - **Phases:** Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
  - **Meiosis:** Division of sex cells (gametes). Results in four non-identical daughter cells with half the chromosome number.
    - **Phases:** Meiosis I (reduction division) and Meiosis II (similar to mitosis).

## **Genetics and Heredity**

### **Basic Genetics**
- **Cause:** Observation of inheritance patterns in organisms led to the discovery of genetic principles.
- **Effect:** Mendel's work laid the foundation for understanding how traits are passed from parents to offspring.
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Genes:** Units of heredity made up of DNA.
  - **Alleles:** Different forms of a gene (dominant and recessive).
  - **Genotype:** The genetic makeup of an organism.
  - **Phenotype:** The observable traits of an organism.
  - **Homozygous:** Two identical alleles for a gene.
  - **Heterozygous:** Two different alleles for a gene.
- **Key Figure:**
  - **Gregor Mendel:** The father of modern genetics, discovered the basic laws of inheritance using pea plants.

![[Mendelian-Genetics.png|400]]

---

### **DNA and Heredity**
- **Cause:** The discovery of the structure of DNA enabled scientists to understand genetic inheritance more clearly.
- **Effect:** DNA's role as the carrier of genetic information was confirmed, leading to advances in biotechnology.
- **Key Components of DNA:**
  - **Nucleotides:** The building blocks of DNA, consisting of a sugar, phosphate group, and nitrogenous base.
  - **Double Helix Structure:** DNA's structure, composed of two strands coiled together.
  - **Base Pairing:** Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T), and Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G).
- **DNA Replication:** The process of copying DNA before cell division to ensure daughter cells receive identical genetic information.

## **Ecology and Evolution**

### **Principles of Ecology**
- **Cause:** The need to understand interactions between organisms and their environment led to the development of ecology.
- **Effect:** Insights into ecosystems and energy flow enhanced conservation efforts and environmental protection.
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Ecosystem:** A community of organisms interacting with each other and their environment.
  - **Food Chains and Food Webs:** Represent the flow of energy through ecosystems, with producers, consumers, and decomposers.
  - **Biomes:** Large ecosystems characterized by specific climates and species (e.g., tundra, rainforest, desert).
  - **Energy Flow:** Energy from the sun is captured by producers and passed through trophic levels in the food chain.
- **Key Interactions:**
  - **Predation:** One organism hunts and consumes another.
  - **Mutualism:** Both organisms benefit from the interaction.
  - **Competition:** Organisms vie for the same resources.
  - **Symbiosis:** Close, long-term interactions between species.

![[Ecosystem-Components-Of-Ecosystem.png|500]]

### **Evolution**
- **Cause:** The need to explain the diversity of life on Earth and the origin of species.
- **Effect:** Evolution theory provided a scientific explanation for species development over time.
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Natural Selection:** The process by which organisms with favorable traits are more likely to survive and reproduce.
  - **Adaptation:** A trait that increases an organism's chances of survival in its environment.
  - **Speciation:** The formation of new and distinct species through evolution.
  - **Genetic Drift:** Random changes in allele frequencies in a population.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Charles Darwin:** Developed the theory of natural selection and wrote *On the Origin of Species*.
  - **Alfred Russel Wallace:** Co-discovered the theory of evolution by natural selection.

## **Human Biology**
### **The Human Body Systems**
- **Cause:** The need to understand how the body functions and maintains homeostasis.
- **Effect:** A deeper understanding of body systems helps with disease prevention and medical advancements.
- **Key Systems:**
  - **Circulatory System:** Transports oxygen, nutrients, and waste products throughout the body.
    - Key Organs: Heart, blood vessels.
  - **Respiratory System:** Facilitates gas exchange, taking in oxygen and expelling carbon dioxide.
    - Key Organs: Lungs, trachea.
  - **Digestive System:** Breaks down food to extract nutrients and expel waste.
    - Key Organs: Stomach, intestines, liver.
  - **Musculoskeletal System:** Provides structure and facilitates movement.
    - Key Organs: Bones, muscles, joints.
  - **Nervous System:** Controls and coordinates body functions and responses to stimuli.
    - Key Organs: Brain, spinal cord, nerves.
  - **Endocrine System:** Regulates body processes through hormones.
    - Key Organs: Glands like the thyroid, adrenal glands, pancreas.
  - **Immune System:** Defends the body against pathogens.
    - Key Organs: White blood cells, spleen, lymph nodes.

![[body-system.jpg]]

---

### **Homeostasis**
- **Cause:** The need for organisms to maintain a stable internal environment to survive.
- **Effect:** Systems in the body work together to regulate temperature, pH, water balance, and other vital conditions.
- **Key Processes:**
  - **Negative Feedback:** A mechanism that counteracts changes in the body to maintain stability (e.g., body temperature regulation).
  - **Positive Feedback:** A process that amplifies changes (e.g., childbirth contractions).

## **Biotechnology**
### **Genetic Engineering**
- **Cause:** Advances in genetic research led to the ability to manipulate genes for beneficial purposes.
- **Effect:** Genetic engineering has led to medical advancements, such as gene therapy and genetically modified organisms (GMOs).
- **Applications:**
  - **Gene Therapy:** Inserting genes into a person's cells to treat or prevent disease.
  - **GMOs:** Organisms whose genetic material has been altered, such as crops resistant to pests.
- **Key Techniques:**
  - **CRISPR:** A tool for editing genes with precision.
  - **Cloning:** Creating genetically identical organisms, such as Dolly the sheep (the first cloned mammal).

![[Genetics.webp|600]]

---

### **Genomics**
- **Cause:** The mapping of the human genome has unlocked vast potential for personalized medicine and understanding human biology.
- **Effect:** The ability to sequence and analyze entire genomes provides insights into health, disease, and evolution.
- **Key Events:**
  - **Human Genome Project (1990-2003):** Mapped the human DNA sequence, offering insights into genetic diseases and development.
  - **Personalized Medicine:** Treatments tailored to an individual's genetic profile.

## **Human Impact on the Environment**
- **Cause:** Human activities have caused significant changes to the environment.
- **Effect:** These changes have led to issues like climate change, pollution, and loss of biodiversity.
- **Key Issues:**
  - **Climate Change:** Caused by the buildup of greenhouse gases, leading to global warming.
  - **Deforestation:** The destruction of forests for agriculture and urbanization.
  - **Pollution:** Release of harmful substances into the air, water, and soil.
  - **Loss of Biodiversity:** Extinction of species due to habitat loss, climate change, and over-exploitation.
