## **Key Documents**

### **Articles of Confederation (1781)**  
- **Overview:**  
  The first governing document of the United States, ratified during the Revolutionary War. It created a weak central government and left most powers to the states.  

- **Key Features:**  
  - Unicameral legislature with equal representation for each state.  
  - No executive or judicial branches.  
  - Congress could declare war, sign treaties, and manage foreign affairs but lacked power to levy taxes or regulate commerce.  

- **Weaknesses:**  
  - Inability to collect taxes led to financial instability.  
  - No power to enforce laws or resolve disputes between states.  
  - Prompted events like **Shays' Rebellion (1786–1787)**, highlighting the need for a stronger federal government.  

- **Outcome:**  
  Replaced by the U.S. Constitution in 1787.  

---

### **Constitution of the United States (1787)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Established the framework for the federal government, replacing the Articles of Confederation. It is the supreme law of the United States.  

- **Key Features:**  
  - **Three Branches:**  
    - **Legislative (Congress):** Makes laws.  
    - **Executive (President):** Enforces laws.  
    - **Judicial (Supreme Court):** Interprets laws.  
  - **Checks and Balances:** Prevent any branch from becoming too powerful.  
  - **Federalism:** Division of powers between national and state governments.  

- **Key Compromises in Its Creation:**  
  - **Great Compromise:** Combined elements of the Virginia and New Jersey Plans, creating a bicameral legislature (House and Senate).  
  - **Three-Fifths Compromise:** Counted three-fifths of the enslaved population for representation and taxation.  

---

### **Declaration of Independence (1776)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Written primarily by **Thomas Jefferson**, it declared the colonies' independence from Britain and outlined the principles of self-governance.  

- **Key Principles:**  
  - All men are created equal.  
  - Rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.  
  - Government derives power from the consent of the governed.  

- **Impact:**  
  Sparked the Revolutionary War and inspired other nations to seek independence.  

---

### **Bill of Rights (1791)**  
- **Overview:**  
  The first ten amendments to the U.S. Constitution, guaranteeing fundamental rights and freedoms.  

- **Key Protections:**  
  - **1st Amendment:** Freedom of speech, religion, press, assembly, and petition.  
  - **2nd Amendment:** Right to bear arms.  
  - **5th Amendment:** Protection against self-incrimination and double jeopardy.  
  - **10th Amendment:** Powers not delegated to the federal government are reserved for the states.  

---

### **Emancipation Proclamation (1863)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Issued by President **Abraham Lincoln** during the Civil War, it declared the freedom of all enslaved people in Confederate-held territory.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Strengthened the Union’s moral cause.  
  - Encouraged enslaved people to escape and join Union forces.  
  - Paved the way for the **13th Amendment**, which abolished slavery.  

---

## **Key Doctrines**

### **Monroe Doctrine (1823)**  
- **Overview:**  
  A U.S. foreign policy statement by President **James Monroe** opposing European interference in the Americas.  

- **Key Points:**  
  - The Western Hemisphere is closed to new European colonization.  
  - Any European intervention in the Americas would be seen as a threat to U.S. security.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Asserted U.S. influence in the Americas.  
  - Later used to justify U.S. intervention in Latin America.  

---

### **Truman Doctrine (1947)**  
- **Overview:**  
  A policy to contain communism by providing military and economic aid to countries resisting Soviet influence.  

- **Key Applications:**  
  - U.S. aid to Greece and Turkey to resist communist movements.  
  - Foundation for the policy of containment during the Cold War.  

---

## **Key Compromises**

### **Missouri Compromise (1820)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Addressed the balance of free and slave states in the Union.  

- **Key Terms:**  
  - Missouri admitted as a slave state.  
  - Maine admitted as a free state.  
  - Slavery prohibited in territories north of the 36°30′ latitude line.  

- **Impact:**  
  Temporarily eased tensions but foreshadowed future conflicts over slavery.  

---

### **Compromise of 1850**  
- **Overview:**  
  Aimed to resolve disputes over slavery in newly acquired territories after the Mexican-American War.  

- **Key Provisions:**  
  - California admitted as a free state.  
  - Popular sovereignty would decide slavery in Utah and New Mexico territories.  
  - Fugitive Slave Act strengthened, requiring the return of escaped enslaved people.  

- **Impact:**  
  Increased sectional tensions, particularly over the Fugitive Slave Act.  

---

## **Key Acts**

### **Indian Removal Act (1830)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Signed by President **Andrew Jackson**, it authorized the forced relocation of Native American tribes from their ancestral lands to territories west of the Mississippi River.  

- **Key Impact:**  
  - Led to the **Trail of Tears**, during which thousands of Native Americans died during forced marches.  
  - Opened up millions of acres of land for settlement by white settlers.  

---

### **Homestead Act (1862)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Granted 160 acres of public land to settlers willing to farm it for at least five years.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Encouraged westward expansion.  
  - Helped establish farming communities across the Great Plains.  

---

### **Fugitive Slave Act (1850)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Strengthened laws requiring the return of escaped enslaved people to their enslavers.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Fueled abolitionist movements in the North.  
  - Increased tensions between free and slave states.  

---

### **Civil Rights Act (1964)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Landmark legislation that outlawed discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.  

- **Key Provisions:**  
  - Ended segregation in public places.  
  - Banned employment discrimination.  

- **Impact:**  
  Marked a major victory for the Civil Rights Movement.  

---

## **Key Amendments**

### **13th Amendment (1865)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Abolished slavery and involuntary servitude, except as punishment for a crime.  

- **Impact:**  
  Formally ended slavery in the United States.  

---

### **14th Amendment (1868)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Granted citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the U.S., including formerly enslaved people.  

- **Key Provisions:**  
  - Equal protection under the law.  
  - Due process rights.  

- **Impact:**  
  Became the basis for civil rights advancements.  

---

### **19th Amendment (1920)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Granted women the right to vote.  

- **Impact:**  
  Marked a major victory for the women’s suffrage movement.  

---

## **Key Treaties**

### **Treaty of Paris (1783)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Ended the Revolutionary War and recognized U.S. independence.  

- **Key Terms:**  
  - Britain acknowledged U.S. sovereignty.  
  - Established U.S. borders from the Atlantic Ocean to the Mississippi River.  

---

### **Louisiana Purchase Treaty (1803)**  
- **Overview:**  
  The U.S. purchased the Louisiana Territory from France for $15 million.  

- **Impact:**  
  - Doubled U.S. territory.  
  - Opened the west for exploration and settlement.  

---

### **Treaty of Versailles (1919)**  
- **Overview:**  
  Ended World War I but was not ratified by the U.S.  

- **Impact:**  
  - U.S. refusal to join the League of Nations signaled a return to isolationism.  
  - Set the stage for future conflicts, including World War II.
