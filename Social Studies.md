# Notes: History of the United States of America

## **Formation and Revolutionary Beginnings**
- **Cause:** Tensions between the colonies and Britain over issues like taxation without representation and restrictions on trade.
- **Effect:** The American colonies declared independence, forming the **United States of America**.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **George Washington:** First president, led Continental Army.
  - **Thomas Jefferson:** Principal author of the Declaration of Independence.
- **Event:**
  - **Declaration of Independence (1776):** Formalized the colonies' break from Britain.
  - **Outcome:** The Declaration led to the Revolutionary War against Britain.

---

## **Pre-Colonial Era**
- **Cause:** Indigenous tribes settled across North America before European exploration.
- **Effect:** Native cultures flourished with diverse agricultural, hunting, and trade practices.
- **Key Tribes:**
  - **Mississippian Culture:** Known for creating large earthen mounds and organized urban centers.
  - **Iroquois Confederacy:** A political alliance of six tribes, offering lessons in governance and democracy.

---

## **European Exploration**
- **Cause:** European countries sought new trade routes and territories.
- **Effect:** The arrival of Europeans changed Native American societies, leading to conflict, disease, and displacement.
- **Key Explorers:**
  - **Christopher Columbus (1492):** Sponsored by Spain, discovered the Americas while searching for a western route to Asia.
  - **John Cabot (1497):** An Italian explorer sailing for England, reached the North American coast.
- **Significant Effects:**
  - The **Columbian Exchange** began, transferring goods, culture, and diseases between the Old and New Worlds.
  - Native American populations declined drastically due to diseases like smallpox, which they had no immunity against.

---

## **Colonial Conflicts**
- **Cause:** Disputes between the colonies and Britain over taxation and autonomy.
- **Effect:** Colonial resistance led to the Revolutionary War.
- **Key Events:**
  - **French-Indian War (1754–1763):** Conflict between Britain and France for control over North America. Britain won but faced massive war debts, leading to increased taxes on the colonies.
  - **Boston Tea Party (1773):** Colonists, protesting the Tea Act, dumped British tea into Boston Harbor.
- **Outcome:** The war ended with the **Treaty of Paris 1763**, which resulted in Britain gaining French territories but heavily taxing the colonies, eventually sparking rebellion.

---

## **Revolutionary War (1775–1783)**
- **Cause:** Tensions over taxation, lack of political representation, and a desire for independence.
- **Effect:** The U.S. gained independence, establishing a new form of government based on republican ideals.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **George Washington:** Commander of the Continental Army.
  - **Benjamin Franklin:** Secured French support during the war.
- **Key Allies:**
  - **France:** Provided crucial military support, including troops, naval assistance, and financial aid.
  - **Spain and the Netherlands:** Offered indirect aid to the American cause.
- **Event:**
  - **Battle of Saratoga (1777):** A decisive American victory that convinced France to officially ally with the United States.
- **Outcome:** The Treaty of Paris 1783 recognized U.S. independence and granted significant territorial gains.

---

## **Expansion and Manifest Destiny**
- **Cause:** The belief that the U.S. was destined to expand across North America, from the Atlantic to the Pacific.
- **Effect:** Expansion led to conflict with Native Americans, Mexico, and other nations.
- **Key Events:**
  - **Louisiana Purchase (1803):** France sold territory to the U.S., doubling its size.
  - **Mexican-American War (1846–1848):** The U.S. gained territories like California, Arizona, and New Mexico, though it deepened divisions over slavery.
  - **California Gold Rush (1848–1855):** Dramatically increased the U.S. population and economy.
- **Effect:** The territories acquired led to the expansion of slavery in the South and heightened tensions between the North and South.

---

## **Civil War (1861–1865)**
- **Cause:** Disagreements over slavery, state's rights, and economic differences between North and South.
- **Effect:** The Union (North) defeated the Confederacy (South), preserving the nation and leading to the abolition of slavery.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Abraham Lincoln:** President during the Civil War, issued the **Emancipation Proclamation**.
  - **Robert E. Lee:** General of the Confederate Army.
  - **Ulysses S. Grant:** General of the Union Army, later became president.
- **Key Allies:**
  - **Great Britain and France:** While not officially allied with the Confederacy, both had economic interests in the South's cotton exports.
- **Key Events:**
  - **Battle of Gettysburg (1863):** Turning point in the war, a major Union victory.
  - **Emancipation Proclamation (1863):** Freed slaves in Confederate-held territories.
- **Outcome:** The Confederacy surrendered at **Appomattox Courthouse (1865)**, and the **13th Amendment** abolished slavery.
- **Aftermath:** Reconstruction began in the South, but racial tensions and segregation persisted.

---

## **Progressive Era (1890s–1920s)**
- **Cause:** Industrialization, urbanization, and political corruption spurred calls for reform.
- **Effect:** Significant political and social changes aimed at correcting economic inequalities, social injustices, and improving public welfare.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Theodore Roosevelt:** Advocate for progressive reforms, including trust-busting and conservation.
  - **Woodrow Wilson:** Expanded the role of government in regulating the economy.
- **Key Reforms:**
  - **Labor Rights:** Minimum wage laws, child labor restrictions, and better working conditions.
  - **Women's Suffrage:** The **19th Amendment** (1920) granted women the right to vote.
  - **Prohibition:** The **18th Amendment** (1920) banned the sale of alcohol, leading to the rise of organized crime.
  - **Conservation Efforts:** Roosevelt expanded national parks, forests, and wildlife refuges, creating the foundation of modern environmental policy.

---

## **Civil Rights Movements**
- **Cause:** African Americans, women, and other minority groups fought for equality and an end to discrimination.
- **Effect:** Major legal and social reforms took place, though full equality was not achieved for decades.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Martin Luther King Jr.:** Leader of the civil rights movement, advocating for nonviolent protests.
  - **Rosa Parks:** Her arrest for refusing to give up her bus seat sparked the **Montgomery Bus Boycott**.
- **Key Legal Events:**
  - **Brown v. Board of Education (1954):** Supreme Court ruling that declared segregated schools unconstitutional.
  - **Civil Rights Act of 1964:** Legislation that outlawed discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
  - **Voting Rights Act of 1965:** Removed barriers to voting for African Americans.
- **Key Movements:**
  - **Women’s Rights:** The **Seneca Falls Convention (1848)** launched the movement for women's suffrage.
  - **LGBTQ Rights:** Sparked by the **Stonewall Riots (1969)**, leading to significant social and legal reforms.
- **Effect:** The **Obergefell v. Hodges** decision in 2015 legalized same-sex marriage.

---

## **Problems with Alcohol and Social Issues**
- **Cause:** The temperance movement aimed to curb alcohol consumption, citing its social and moral consequences.
- **Effect:** Prohibition laws were enacted, but they led to unintended consequences like the rise of organized crime.
- **Key Events:**
  - **18th Amendment (1920):** Instituted Prohibition, making alcohol illegal.
  - **21st Amendment (1933):** Repealed Prohibition, acknowledging its failure.
- **Key Figures:**
  - **Carrie Nation:** Famous for using a hatchet to destroy bar property during her temperance activism.
  - **Al Capone:** Infamous mobster who profited from bootlegging alcohol during Prohibition.

---

## **20th Century Global Conflicts**
- **World War I (1914–1918):**
  - **Cause:** Nationalism, imperialism, militarism, and entangled alliances caused the war.
  - **Effect:** The U.S. helped secure an Allied victory, leading to the Treaty of Versailles.
  - **Allies:** France, Britain, Italy, and later the U.S. faced off against the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire).
- **World War II (1939–1945):**
  - **Cause:** Aggression from Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, Japan) and their expansionism.
  - **Effect:** The U.S. became a global superpower, the U.N. was founded, and the Cold War began.
  - **Key Allies:** The U.S., the Soviet Union, and the United Kingdom defeated Germany, Italy, and Japan.

---

## **Modern Foundations**
- **Cause:** Continued expansion, industrialization, and efforts to secure rights for all citizens.
- **Effect:** The United States became a global economic and military leader.
- **Key Events:**
  - **Constitution and Bill of Rights:** Ensured the foundation of civil liberties and governance.
  - **Immigration:** The U.S. remains a "melting pot," contributing to cultural and economic richness.
